# Activity 5 — Build a GHG Governance, Compliance and Risk Plan

**Alignment:** K6 · K7 · A4 · A8 · LO2  
**Duration:** 60 minutes  
**Objective:** Design roles, controls, compliance monitoring and mitigation actions for material GHG risks.

## Scenario

EcoForge faces lender reporting covenants, customer product-footprint requests, Singapore energy and carbon-cost exposure, flood risk at the Johor warehouse, supplier transition risk and reputational risk from unsupported green claims. Responsibility is fragmented across Finance, Facilities, Procurement and Sustainability.

## Materials
- Obligation and stakeholder fact sheet
- Climate-risk event cards
- Current role descriptions
- Risk-control workbook

## Questions
- Which requirements are mandatory, contractual or voluntary?
- Who is accountable for inventory approval and source controls?
- Which risks need treatment, monitoring or escalation?
- What indicators provide early warning?

## Detailed procedure
1. Create an obligations register showing source, applicability, owner, required output, frequency and evidence.
2. Map decision rights using responsible, accountable, consulted and informed roles for data, methods, review and claims.
3. Describe each GHG-related risk as cause, event and consequence rather than a vague topic.
4. Assess likelihood, consequence, velocity and current-control effectiveness using documented criteria.
5. Select mitigation actions following avoid, reduce, transfer/share and accept logic.
6. Define preventive, detective and corrective controls for high-priority inventory and transition risks.
7. Choose monitoring indicators, thresholds, owners and escalation routes.
8. Test the plan against a flood disruption and a customer challenge to an emissions claim.
9. Present a concise governance recommendation to the executive sponsor.

## Required evidence
- Compliance register
- GHG governance RACI
- Risk and control register
- Monitoring dashboard and escalation protocol

## Acceptance check

Every applicable obligation has an owner and evidence; high risks have proportionate controls and indicators; accountability and escalation are unambiguous.
